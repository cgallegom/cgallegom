**Economist** · applied microeconometrics and machine learning for health and public policy

**Website: [cgallegom.github.io](https://cgallegom.github.io)**

PhD candidate in the interuniversity doctoral programme in Economics, Management and Organization of the Universidad Pública de Navarra (UPNA), the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona (UAB) and the Universitat de les Illes Balears (UIB), expected in 2026. Research Assistant in the Department of Econometrics, Statistics and Applied Economics at the Universitat de Barcelona.

I use applied microeconometrics and machine learning as methodological tools to answer specific questions in economics, especially in health and public policy. Each question calls for a different approach: description for monitoring, prediction for planning and counterfactual inference for intervention. Machine learning is particularly useful with high-dimensional data, unknown relationships and multiple interactions, while microeconometric designs identify causal effects. I apply them to the quality and efficiency of hospital care, the use of health services and the effects of system-wide shocks such as COVID-19 on primary care, using large administrative health datasets from Spain and Catalonia.

## Doctoral thesis

*Adopting Machine Learning in Health Economics: International Review and Empirical Evidence from Catalonia*

The thesis applies and evaluates machine learning methods for health economics questions using Spanish and Catalan health data. It maps what the applied literature on machine-learning prediction of healthcare utilisation does and omits, predicts and interprets avoidable hospitalisations across primary care teams, and estimates the effect of hospital ownership on the quality and efficiency of elective surgery. [Read the full abstract](https://cgallegom.github.io/research.html#doctoral-thesis).

---

## Research agenda

- **Hospital quality, efficiency and ownership.** Differences in outcomes and efficiency between public and private hospitals, including elective surgery in Catalonia.
- **Primary care and system shocks.** Effects of the intensity of COVID-19 on primary care models and outcomes, using continuous difference-in-differences designs.
- **Healthcare utilisation and avoidable hospitalisations.** Explaining variation across patients and providers, with interpretable machine learning as a complement to econometric analysis.
- **Multimorbidity, ageing and resource use.** The burden and health-care costs of chronic and multiple long-term conditions.
